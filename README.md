# Online Shoppers' Purchasing Intentions (Excel)

**The Data:** The dataset, sourced from ([UC Irvine](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/468/online+shoppers+purchasing+intention+dataset)), is a collection of data related to purchase patterns & consumer behavior in the context of online shopping. It was created by conducting surveys of online shoppers & collecting data from their responses. It contains information including the number of pages visited during sessions, how much time was spent per page, bounce rates & exit rates, the month, whether the customer was new or returning, & whether a purchase was made or not. The raw version of the data is available in the "data_orig" worksheet in the Excel file. There are 12,330 data points.  
By analyzing this data, valuable insights can be obtained that can inform companies & websites as to trends of their online customers & what factors are more important in how they interact with the site such as whether they make purchases or not. Such information can help guide actions regarding a company's website in how they make adjustments to it as well as how they choose to advertise & market themselves to customers.

This project was done in May, 2025. In the Excel file, there are five worksheets containing the following: notes on the dataset, the raw dataset, a cleaner, modified version of the data, analyses including pivot tables & visuals, & a dashboard containing important metrics & figures ("Dashboard (1)").

### Brief Summary
This project examined information on purchasing patterns & consumer behavior of customers, as reported by themselves, when they shop online. It is based within an 11-month span. Various metrics & figures were assembled including the number of sessions that saw or did not see a purchase, the average times spent on different page types & whether purchases were made, bounce rates by purchase group, sessions by visitor type & purchase group, monthly sessions that saw a purchase or not, & more.

The original version of the dataset required no preprocessing. Additional variables were built indicating the total number of pages visited by a user, how much time they spent in their session, & the average amounts of time they spent per page across each of the three different page types--administrative, informational, & product-related.

Of the 12,330 sessions, 1,908 (~ 16%) reported a purchase, whereas 10,422 did not. Generally, people spent most of their sessions on product-related pages & the least on informational pages. Of those who made a purchase, they spent slightly less time (by about two minutes) on product-related pages compared to people who did not make a purchase, but they spent considerably more time on administrative & informational pages (by about ten minutes each).  
When extrapolated to whether the session occurred during the week or weekend, customers spent more time on the pages when they made purchases than when they didn't either way. For people who made a purchase, they spent slightly more time on product-related & administrative pages & less on informational pages during the week compared to the weekend. In sessions that did not result in a purchase, product-related pages were also viewed for longer during the week, but these customers spent more time on administrative & informational pages during the weekends than the week.

Of the 10,422 sessions that did not see a purchase, the user bounced--when a user enters & leaves a website without viewing any other pages--about 57% of the time. In sessions that reported purchases, this rate was about 46%, indicating that customers who made purchases typically bounced less often & interacted with the website more often than those who did not.

Data was recorded between February & December with the exception of April. The most sessions occurred in May, November, March, & December. No months saw more sessions with a purchase than sessions without one. The period between June-October was relatively quiet in terms of total reported sessions; however, more sessions were observed each subsequent month & up through November. The months that saw the greatest percentages of sessions in which purchases were made were November (about a quarter of the sessions saw a purchase), October (~ 21%), & September (~ 19%). Between February-June, no more than 11% of the monthly sessions resulted in purchases.

Even though most sessions were logged by returning customers, new customers made purchases in about 11% more of their sessions; about one in every four sessions resulted in a purchase amongst new customers. Additionally, customers made purchases slightly more often (by about 2.5%) on weekends than during the week.
